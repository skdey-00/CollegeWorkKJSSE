# =================== DEMO PAPER 1 ===================

**Q1. Attempt any two:** *(2×5=10)*
(i) Write a short note on the **diaphragm pressure gauge** with a suitable diagram.
(ii) State **Pascal's law** with the equation. Give two industrial applications.
(i) — (iii) Two pistons of a hydraulic lift have diameters 40 cm and 4 cm. What force on the large piston is created by 20 N on the small one?

**Q2. Attempt any one:** *(10)*
(i) Explain **gear pumps and vane pumps** with diagrams: construction, working, advantages, disadvantages.
(ii) Explain the function of **accumulators** in fluid power systems. List the types; explain the bladder type with a diagram.

**Q3. Attempt any one:** *(10)*
(i) Explain the **pressure relief valve** with a diagram. Draw a circuit where it protects a pump and explain the working.
(ii) Draw the **meter-in circuit** and explain. Where is meter-out preferred over meter-in?

**Q4. Attempt the following:** *(5+5=10)*
(i) Draw the pneumatic circuit for **direct control of a single-acting cylinder**. Name all components.
(ii) Explain the **shuttle valve** with symbol and one application.

**Q5. Attempt the following:** *(5+5=10)*
(i) Explain **latching in electrical circuits** with a diagram.
(ii) Draw the electro-pneumatic circuit for **AND logic** control of a single-acting cylinder (two push buttons must both be pressed).

---KEY (Demo 1)---
Q1(iii): F = 20×(40/4)² = 2000 N.
Q2(i): gear pump — two meshing gears, oil carried in tooth spaces, 20–175 bar typical, cheap, noisy; vane pump — rotor with sliding vanes in cam ring, quieter, variable displacement versions.
Q2(ii): accumulator stores energy / damps pulsations / emergency supply / leakage compensation; types: weighted, spring, gas (bladder, diaphragm, piston).
Q3(i): relief = normally closed, pilot line senses pump pressure, opens at set value to tank; sketch per Module 3 slides.
Q3(ii): meter-in: FCV in the supply line to cylinder — controls extend speed for resistive loads; meter-out for overrunning/negative loads (presses, hanging loads).
Q4(i): 3/2 way valve (solenoid or push-button) + single-acting cylinder + air supply via FRL; spring return.
Q4(ii): shuttle = two inlets one outlet, higher pressure input wins → OR logic; application: safety — control from either of two stations.
Q5(i): relay NO contact parallel with the momentary PB keeps coil energized after release; stop PB (NC) breaks it.
Q5(ii): two 3/2 PBs in SERIES (electrical AND) energizing relay → relay contact switches solenoid of 3/2 valve → cylinder extends.

# =================== DEMO PAPER 2 ===================

**Q1. Attempt any two:** *(2×5=10)*
(i) Compare **hydraulic and pneumatic systems** (min 5 points: medium, pressure range, speed, precision, cost, safety).
(ii) Explain the **FRL unit** — function of each element.
(iii) State the **continuity equation** and write its two uses.

**Q2. Attempt any one:** *(10)*
(i) Explain **piston pumps** (axial/swash-plate) — construction, working, adv/disadv; compare with plunger pumps.
(ii) What are **intensifiers**? Explain types with diagrams; give the pressure-intensification relation.

**Q3. Attempt any one:** *(10)*
(i) Explain the **sequence valve** with diagram; draw a **sequencing circuit** (two cylinders: clamp then press) and explain.
(ii) Draw and explain the **pressure-compensated flow control valve**; why is it preferred over a needle valve?

**Q4. Attempt the following:** *(5+5=10)*
(i) Draw the pneumatic circuit for **automatic reciprocation (oscillatory motion) of a double-acting cylinder** using roller-lever valves.
(ii) Explain the **time-delay valve** with symbol; one application.

**Q5. Attempt the following:** *(5+5=10)*
(i) Explain construction, working and advantages of **relays** with diagram.
(ii) Draw the electro-pneumatic circuit for **automatic return of a double-acting cylinder**: extend on PB, auto-return at forward end via limit switch.

---KEY (Demo 2)---
Q1(i): table medium oil vs air; 70–350 bar vs 2–8 bar... (per Module 1.2 slides).
Q2(i): axial piston — cylinder block on swash plate, reciprocating pistons, high pressure 300+ bar, efficient, costlier; plunger = single reciprocating rod, seal issues, simple.
Q2(ii): P2 = P1(A1/A2); types: single-stage, two-stage air-oil intensifiers per slides.
Q3(i): sequence valve opens when inlet reaches set pressure → directs flow to secondary actuator; clamp-then-press circuit: cylinder A (clamp) → sequence valve → cylinder B (press).
Q3(ii): pressure compensator holds ΔP across throttle constant → flow independent of load; needle valve flow varies with load.
Q4(i): 5/2 pilot valve, roller-levers at both ends of cylinder trip the pilot lines → continuous to-and-fro.
Q4(ii): one-way FCV + accumulator/relief chamber = air reservoir charging to set pressure then switching signal; used for dwell/timed sequences.
Q5(ii): PB (3/2) → solenoid Y1 of 5/2 double-solenoid valve; limit switch at forward end → Y2 → auto retract.
